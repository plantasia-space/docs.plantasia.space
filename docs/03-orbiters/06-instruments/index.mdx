An **instrument** makes the sound. It sits at the head of a <Term id="world-dimension">World dimension</Term>'s chain: whatever comes
after it (audio effects, the <Term id="star">Star</Term>'s mixer, a <Term id="moon">Moon</Term>) hears it, and whatever comes before it (a
generator, MIDI effects) tells it which notes to play.

## Audio or Notes

What World **I** holds decides what kind of Orbiter you are building:

| | World I plays… | World II and III | Instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Audio Orbiter** | the audio you choose, on the **Track player** or **Granular** | audio effects that shape it | one |
| **Notes Orbiter** | a notes instrument, such as the **Sampler**, the **Subtractive synth** or the **FM synth** | one more notes instrument each, if you like | up to three |

:::note[Audio or Notes, never both]

An Orbiter is one or the other. Replacing World I's instrument with one of the other kind switches the Orbiter, and the Studio asks first. See [Multiple MIDI instruments](/docs/orbiters/instruments/multiple-midi-instruments) for how a Notes Orbiter plays two or three instruments side by side.

:::

## What every instrument shares

- **It is built in the Studio.** Pick it in the **Instrument** step, or from the module picker of a
  World dimension.
- **Its knobs map to the Orbiter.** Any knob or switch can follow an <Term id="axis">axis</Term> (X, Y, Z) or a toggle, with
  its own minimum, <Term id="equilibrium">equilibrium</Term> and maximum. See [Edit](/docs/orbiters/edit).
- **It shows its sound on a screen.** Each instrument has a small screen in its panel; what it draws
  is described on the instrument's page.
- **A notes instrument plays whatever notes reach it**, from the keys, MIDI, the piano roll or a
  sequencer. In the chain it sits after any generator and MIDI effects, and before the audio effects.

### Envelopes

An envelope is drawn above its knobs: **Attack**, **Decay**, **Sustain** and **Release**. Drag a
point, or type a value under it. Attack, Decay and Release can each be **bent**: the small hollow
handle in the middle of a sloped segment is its curve. Drag it across the segment, from **−100 %** to
**+100 %**: **+** is fast at the start (a quick rise, a quick drop and a long tail), **−** slow (a
swell, a fall that holds before it goes). Double-click a handle to straighten it. A curve can be
mapped to an axis like any other control.

### LFO and Voice

Most notes instruments have these two groups, folded at first:

- **LFO**: a slow wave that moves things by itself. **Sync** keeps it on the Orbiter's beat (a note
  value or bars); off, **Rate** is in Hz. Six shapes, and **Amount**: how much of it gets through.
  What it moves is set in the [Matrix](#the-matrix).
- **Voice**: **Voices** (how many notes sound at once, 1 … 16), **Mono** (one note at a time) and
  **Legato** (with Mono, a note played over a held one slides there without a new strike, over
  **Glide**).

### In a room

Everyone in a room hears the same notes and the same sound. Only what has no fixed start can differ a
little between players: noise, and a free (unsynced) LFO over a long take. The pitch bend, the
pressure and the mod wheel stay on the device that plays them.

## The Matrix

The **Matrix** is where you say what moves a knob: velocity, the mod wheel, an LFO. The Sampler, the
Subtractive synth, the FM synth, the Analog synth, the Wavetable synth and Pitched percussion share
it: a group in the panel that starts folded. It is a grid: what moves things across the top, what is
moved down the side, an amount in each cell.

It has two tabs:

- **Modulation**: the instrument's own LFOs and envelopes across the top. An instrument with none
  (Pitched percussion) has no such tab.
- **MIDI**: what you play, the same six columns in every instrument.

| Column | What it reads |
|---|---|
| **Vel** | The note's velocity. |
| **Key** | Where the note is on the keyboard, around C3. |
| **PB** | The pitch bend wheel. |
| **Press** | The keyboard's pressure (channel aftertouch). |
| **Mod** | The mod wheel. |
| **Rand** | A value drawn once for each note. |

**A cell.** A dot means nothing is routed. Drag a cell left or right to set an amount from
**−100 %** to **+100 %** (hold Shift for finer steps), or use the arrow keys; double-click puts it
back. The arrow in the cell points the way the amount goes. **Key › Cutoff** at 100 % follows the
keyboard exactly. Every cell of a row adds to its knob, and the result never leaves the knob's range.

**The rows.** A tab opens short: the instrument's few main rows, and every row that has an amount.
Turn one of the instrument's knobs while the Matrix is open and its row comes in, if it has one. A
row you leave empty is gone the next time you open the Orbiter. A row's label fills up to where its
knob stands, so you can read the starting point beside the amounts.

Three kinds of row are in the MIDI tab only (the Wavetable synth has no Vibrato or Level row: its LFOs and Gain rows do that work):

- **Pitch** holds the pitch bend: **PB › Pitch** rests at **16.7 %**, two semitones. Set it to 0 and
  the wheel stops bending the pitch; another PB cell makes it move something else.
- **Vibrato** is the instrument's own vibrato, a fixed 5.5 Hz. **Mod › Vibrato** rests at 50 %, so
  the mod wheel does something on a fresh instrument. **Level** is the note's level.
- **LFO** (or **LFO 1** and **LFO 2**) is that LFO's **Amount** knob, which scales everything the LFO
  moves. Amount at 0 % with **Mod › LFO** at 100 % is the wheel bringing the LFO in. Only PB, Press
  and Mod reach an LFO row.

Like any number, a cell can go on an <Term id="axis">axis</Term> in the Studio's **Map** mode: the axis then sets *how
much* the source moves its row.
