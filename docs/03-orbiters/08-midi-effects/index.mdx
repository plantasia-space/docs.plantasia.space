**MIDI effects** work on notes, not on sound. They sit **before** the instrument, so they decide what
it plays: which notes, when, and how hard. They belong to a Notes Orbiter (an Orbiter that plays notes with a synth or the Sampler, not an
audio).

<IsoFigure name="orbiters/midi-effects" label="A length of the Orbiter's rack with a pin drum, a box with a grid of notes and a box with a keyboard. A note leaves the drum, moves to another row on the grid, and a key plays it.">
  Move over the rack to follow one note.
</IsoFigure>

| On the rack | In an Orbiter |
|---|---|
| **The pin drum**, turning by itself | a generator: it makes notes on its own, so it leads the chain |
| **The box with the grid** | a MIDI effect: the note comes in on one row and leaves on another |
| **The box with a keyboard** | the instrument, which plays what reaches it |

## Generators and MIDI effects

There are two kinds, and the order is always the same:

1. **A generator** makes notes on its own, so it leads the chain: the **Drum sequencer**, the
   **Arpeggiator**, **Step sequencer**. A dimension has at most one.
2. **MIDI effects** reshape the notes that reach them, from a generator, the keys, MIDI hardware or
   the piano roll: **Note echo**, **Velocity**, **Note length**, **Random**, **Chord**, **Scale**, **Pitch**. The **MIDI
   monitor** changes nothing: it shows what reaches it.

Echoes and repeats land exactly on the beat.

:::tip[MIDI effects load neutral]

Adding one changes nothing until you move something: turn it up while you play. Scale holds the notes to the Orbiter's key as soon as it has one. The generators are the exception: the Step sequencer plays as soon as you add it, and the Arpeggiator as soon as you hold a key.

:::
