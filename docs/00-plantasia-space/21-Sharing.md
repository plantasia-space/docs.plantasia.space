## How Sharing and Permissions Work

plantasia space uses two simple models to control access and collaboration:

- **Audio uses a visibility (audience) model** — focused on listening and release permissions
- **Worlds, Orbiters, and Collections use a collaboration privacy model** — focused on editing, publishing, and teamwork

## Audio Visibility (Audience)

When you publish audio, you choose who can play and discover it:

- **Private** — only you can access it
- **Shared** — anyone with the audio link can view it
- **Public** — fully public and discoverable

This controls playback and distribution.

## Entangled World, Orbiter, and Collection Permissions

These use a collaboration model to decide:

- Who can access the entity directly
- Who can contribute and what role they have

### General Access (Link or Public Rules)

You can also define baseline access for everyone:

- **Restricted** — only invited xPlorers can access
- **Anyone with the link** — access via link with a selected role

Default roles for general access can be:

- Viewer
- Publisher
- Editor

### Who Can Change These Settings

Inviting people and opening an entity up are two different decisions, so they sit with different people. Editors can invite and remove xPlorers and change their roles, since they already edit and publish the entity. Deciding whether anyone with the link can reach it stays with the owner alone, as does archiving or deleting it. Publishers and Viewers can't change collaboration settings.

Clips follow the roles of their Entangled World: the world's owner and editors can edit any Clip in it, and only the world's owner — besides the Clip's maker — can archive or delete one. See [Who can work on a Clip](../02-entangled-worlds/04-studio.md#who-can-work-on-a-clip).

If a setting is not yours to change, you will see it greyed out with a note explaining why, rather than an error after the fact.

Audio has no collaborator list. Its audience is the owner's to set — see [Audio Visibility](#audio-visibility-audience) above.

This model is optimized for collaboration, contributions, and shared creation.

## Important Behavior

- Public audio can use private or shared Worlds, Orbiters, and Collections
- Audio visibility controls who can listen and discover the audio
- Entity privacy controls collaboration rights and direct access

## In Practice

You can:

- Keep a World private so only you can publish in it
- Release public audio that uses that private World
- Let listeners experience it through the audio

Others will not be able to open, edit, or publish inside that World unless you invite them.

### Simple Rule to Remember

**Audio controls who listens.**  
**Worlds, Orbiters, and Collections control who collaborates and publishes.**

## Licences {#licences}

Permissions decide who can open a work **inside** Plantasia Space. A **Creative Commons licence**
decides what anyone may do with it **outside** too. Every work you release carries one:
an Audio, an Orbiter, an Entangled World or a collection.

- **CC BY 4.0**: anyone may use and adapt the work, also commercially, if they credit you.
- **CC BY-NC 4.0**: the same, but not for commercial use. Turn on **Noncommercial** in the
  Licensing & Rights box to choose it.

You keep your copyright either way. Only the owner chooses the licence.

### Works made from other works

- **The strictest licence wins.** If a work adapts someone else's non-commercial work, it is
  non-commercial too. Putting music to moving image, as a Clip does, always counts as adapting it.
  You can always be stricter than the works you use, never less.
- **Credit every author.** A Clip's credits name you, its Entangled World and its Audio, each with
  the author and the licence it was used under.
- **Clips** take the licence of their Entangled World, made non-commercial by someone else's
  non-commercial Audio.
- **Collections**: the licence covers your curation (title, cover, text and order). Each item keeps
  its own licence. Collecting is not adapting: a collection never turns non-commercial because of
  what it holds.
- **Someone else's Orbiter** you play or record with is a work you use: credit its author and
  respect its licence.
- Someone else's work with **no licence yet** can't be used in a Clip until its author chooses one.
  Your own works never block you.

A licence can't be taken back. If you change it later, whoever used an earlier release under the
earlier licence may go on doing so. Each release keeps the licence it was published with.

## Downloading Content

Some audios can be downloaded directly. Downloads are enabled per release by the creator: when publishing or editing an audio, the **Enable Direct Downloads** option allows listeners to download it.

When direct downloads are enabled, the audio page shows a **Download** button. It opens a dialog where you choose the version:

- **Compressed (MP3)** — a smaller file for everyday listening
- **Lossless (WAV)** — full quality for archival use

Released Clips can also offer a video download (original or compressed MP4) from the Clip page — only when the Clip has a captured video, and for two weeks after the capture.

Downloaded files remain covered by the release's Creative Commons license — check the license shown on the content page for what you may do with the file beyond listening.
